Rosiglitazone Response in GIPR-Adip Adipose Tissue

File names use the figure numbers printed in
Artemis_Biolabs_Kusminski_Project1_RosiGIPR.pdf.
Appendix figures continue the same sequence (Figure 13 to Figure 21).

This archive holds every figure as a 300 dpi PNG, including
the two KEGG pathway maps.

Report figures (printed in the PDF)
------------------------------------
Fig01_QC_PCA.png
  Figure 1. Genotype separates the pools on PC1, and two WT Rosi pools carry the strongest skeletal-muscle signal.

Fig02_pipeline.png
  Figure 2. Analysis workflow.

Fig03_volcano_C1_INT.png
  Figure 3. The baseline genotype contrast has 10,379 significant genes, and the interaction 861, led by Chst11, Tsku and Aldh1a1.

Fig04_composition.png
  Figure 4. GIPR-Adip fat has a lower adipocyte signal and a higher myeloid and stromal signal in both treatments.

Fig05_PPARg_benchmark.png
  Figure 5. Rosi-induced 3T3-L1 genes rise in both genotypes, whereas Rosi-repressed genes fall only in WT fat.

Fig06_GSEA.png
  Figure 6. Cell-cycle and matrix sets respond to Rosi only in GIPR-Adip fat, and the matrix signal weakens after composition adjustment.

Fig07_TierA_heatmap.png
  Figure 7. Tier A genes split into a matrix group raised in GIPR-Adip Rosi pools and a myeloid group highest in GIPR-Adip vehicle pools.

Fig08_candidates_baseline.png
  Figure 8. Gipr, Gdf15, Ucp1, Lep and Fgf21 differ mainly at baseline; Rosi raises Gdf15 significantly only in GIPR-Adip fat, and the Smpd3 Rosi response differs by genotype.

Fig09_KEGG_pathway_bars.png
  Figure 9. Rosi enriches oxidative, TCA, thermogenic and PPAR pathways in WT fat more strongly than in GIPR-Adip fat.

Fig10_glycolysis_TCA_PDH_heatmap.png
  Figure 10. GIPR-Adip fat is low on PDH and TCA genes, and Rosi raises them only in WT fat.

Fig11_two_calcium_modules.png
  Figure 11. Atp2a2 and Ryr2 separate by genotype with little pool-to-pool spread, whereas the fast-skeletal genes vary by up to 11.7 log2 units within groups.

Fig12_published_concordance.png
  Figure 12. Genes significant in both datasets agree in direction 92.6% of the time.

Fig13_leave_one_pool_out.png
  Figure 13. Rosi contrasts depend on individual pools more than genotype contrasts do.

Fig14_composition_robustness.png
  Figure 14. Reference donor choice changes few results; the marker set matters more.

Fig15_candidates_calcium_remodeling.png
  Figure 15. Ccl2, Timp1 and Atp2a1 show a genotype-dependent Rosi response, whereas Atp2a2, Ryr2 and S100g differ only at baseline.

Fig16_secreted_TierA.png
  Figure 16. Six secreted Tier A genes respond to Rosi only in GIPR-Adip fat.

Fig17_energy_metabolism_heatmap.png
  Figure 17. Rosi raises fatty-acid oxidation, lipolysis and lipogenesis genes in WT fat, and GIPR-Adip fat is low on these genes in both treatments.

Fig18_radar_energy_pathways.png
  Figure 18. WT Rosi rises on adipocyte metabolic axes, whereas GIPR-Adip fat is low on them and higher on creatine cycling.

Fig19_PPARg_KEGG_heatmap.png
  Figure 19. Some PPAR-signalling genes rise with Rosi in both genotypes, whereas others, including Ucp1 and Cpt1b, rise only in WT fat.

Fig20_muscle_signature.png
  Figure 20. Fast-skeletal genes co-vary with Myh4 across pools; cardiac-type calcium-handling genes do not.

Fig21_myeloid_states.png
  Figure 21. Smpd3 and the baseline GIPR-Adip programme are highest in lipid-associated macrophages, and Gdf15 in lipid-associated and chemokine-high macrophages.
