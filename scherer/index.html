    How to read this project

    This project asked a single, focused question: does rosiglitazone act differently on subcutaneous white fat when GIPR is overexpressed in adipocytes than it does in wild-type fat? Twelve pooled subcutaneous white-fat libraries were sequenced across a complete two-by-two design — genotype (GIPR-Adip versus WT) crossed with treatment (rosiglitazone versus vehicle), three pools per group, all animals on two weeks of doxycycline high-fat diet. That design lets us separate three things that are easy to conflate: the baseline transcriptional effect of adipocyte GIPR overexpression, the transcriptional effect of rosiglitazone within each genotype on its own, and — the actual question of interest — the formal interaction, meaning the set of genes whose rosiglitazone response is genuinely different in GIPR-Adip fat from what the same drug does in WT fat. The headline result is that the baseline genotype effect is very large and dominates the dataset, while the drug-by-genotype interaction is real but a much smaller, secondary layer sitting on top of it. The analysis also tracked a specific mechanistic interest of the lab — GDF15 as a candidate appetite-suppressing secreted factor, and calcium-cycling genes as a candidate thermogenic route — and reports plainly where the data support those ideas and where they do not.

    How we analyzed your data

    Design and pipeline. Twelve bulk RNA-seq libraries were analysed, each library a pool of animals rather than a single mouse: three WT-vehicle pools (2 mice per pool), three GIPR-Adip(GA)-vehicle pools (1 mouse per pool), three WT-rosiglitazone pools (3 mice per pool), and three GA-rosiglitazone pools (1-2 mice per pool). GIPR-Adip animals carry both the TRE-GIPR and adiponectin-rtTA transgenes; WT controls carry at most one of the two alone. All mice received two weeks of doxycycline high-fat diet, so doxycycline exposure is not a variable between groups. Pooling is important to how the results should be read: the pool, not the mouse, is the unit of replication, so each group has n = 3 independent units and within-group biological spread is partly averaged away inside each pool. Quality control, principal-component inspection, and reference-based cell-composition estimation were run before any differential testing, and the composition read-out is reported descriptively alongside the gene-level results rather than being used to redefine them.

    Primary statistical model and sensitivity checks. The primary model is DESeq2 with the design ~0 + group, i.e. a single four-level group factor fitted without an intercept, from which every comparison is drawn as an explicit contrast: C1, GA-vehicle versus WT-vehicle (the baseline genotype effect); C2, rosiglitazone versus vehicle within WT; C3, rosiglitazone versus vehicle within GA; C4, GA versus WT on rosiglitazone; and the genotype x rosiglitazone interaction, defined as the rosiglitazone effect in GA fat minus the rosiglitazone effect in WT fat. Significance throughout is Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p < 0.05, and effect sizes are reported as log2 fold changes with 95% confidence intervals. Because the PCA showed that a subset of pools carries a stronger skeletal-muscle signal — an expected consequence of dissecting subcutaneous depots — several sensitivity analyses were run in parallel: a model adding a muscle-signature covariate, a composition-adjusted model, a voom/quality-weights alternative, subset refits, and leave-one-pool-out refits of each contrast. These exist to tell us which findings are fragile, and every gene-level table carries per-gene robustness flags from them. To be explicit: the muscle-covariate model is a secondary sensitivity check only. It is not the primary model, and the gene counts reported in the findings section come from the primary ~0 + group model.

    Tier A classification and the published-data comparison. Candidate "GIPR-specific rosiglitazone" genes were filtered conservatively rather than ranked by p-value alone. To be called Tier A, a gene had to (i) reach significance in the formal genotype x rosiglitazone interaction test, (ii) show the clean directional pattern of a significant rosiglitazone response within GIPR-Adip fat with no significant rosiglitazone response in WT fat, and (iii) survive both the muscle-covariate and the composition-adjusted sensitivity refits. Every gene in the delivered Tier A list meets all three criteria and carries that same pattern label; twenty of them additionally carry a UniProt "secreted" annotation, which is why they are broken out separately as circulating-factor candidates. For external grounding, the baseline genotype contrast (C1) was compared gene-by-gene against the published baseline GIPR-Adip RNA-seq dataset (PMID 39642881): the two result tables were matched on gene identity, effect sizes were correlated across all shared genes (Pearson and Spearman), and directional agreement was then assessed within the subset of genes called significant in both datasets. This is a concordance check on the baseline genotype signature, not an external replication of the rosiglitazone or interaction results, which have no published counterpart.

    Model summary across contrasts

    Findings

    The baseline genotype effect (C1: GIPR-Adip vehicle versus WT vehicle) is the dominant signal in the dataset: 10,379 significant genes (5,109 up / 5,270 down). Adipocyte GIPR overexpression on doxycycline high-fat diet reshapes the subcutaneous white-fat transcriptome on a scale that is hard to overstate, and this is the single most robust result in the study.

    Rosiglitazone alone behaves very differently in the two genotypes. In WT fat (C2) rosiglitazone moves 2,084 significant genes (939 up / 1,145 down). In GIPR-Adip fat (C3) the same treatment moves 747 significant genes (318 up / 429 down) — a markedly smaller transcriptional response. Read on its own, that difference is descriptive: it is a comparison of two separate within-genotype contrasts, not a formal interaction test, and the two genotypes also differ in how many mice contributed per pool.

    The priority contrast you asked for, GIPR-Adip versus WT both on rosiglitazone (C4), returns 10,493 significant genes (5,156 up / 5,337 down). Critically, this is largely the same signal as the baseline genotype effect rather than a new drug-induced divergence: 81% of C4 genes (8,504 of 10,493) are also significant in C1, and effect sizes across the two contrasts correlate at r = 0.87. In other words, when you compare the genotypes on drug, what you are mostly seeing is the pre-existing genotype difference persisting through treatment.

    The formal genotype x rosiglitazone interaction — the only test that directly asks whether the drug does something different depending on genotype — identifies 861 genes (519 up / 342 down). Applying the conservative filter described in the methods (significant interaction, a rosiglitazone response present in GIPR-Adip fat but absent in WT fat, and survival of both the muscle-covariate and composition-adjusted sensitivity refits) narrows this to 53 robust, GIPR-specific rosiglitazone-responsive genes, twenty of which are annotated as secreted. These 53 are the most defensible short list for follow-up, and they should be read as a secondary layer on top of the dominant baseline genotype effect, not as a rival to it.

    Transgene validation and a fold-change correction

    Gipr induction was quantified directly from the per-pool expression values, restricted to the six GIPR-overexpressing pools and expressed relative to the WT mean of the same treatment arm. Across those six pools, induction ranges from 20.3-fold to 43.0-fold, with a vehicle-group mean of 34.1-fold and a rosiglitazone-group mean of 25.8-fold. These are the correct figures for the transgene; any wider range quoted elsewhere should be disregarded in favour of these per-pool values.

    GDF15

    Gdf15 is strikingly elevated in GIPR-Adip fat at baseline — approximately 26-fold higher (log2FC 4.71, padj 1.6e-61), one of the most significant single-gene results in the study. Rosiglitazone then raises Gdf15 significantly within GIPR-Adip fat (log2FC 1.07, padj 2.3e-5) while the corresponding effect in WT fat does not reach significance (log2FC 0.68, padj 0.16). That pattern is suggestive, but the formal interaction test for Gdf15 is not significant (log2FC 0.40, padj 0.69). Stated plainly: these data do not demonstrate that rosiglitazone affects GDF15 differently depending on genotype. The attractive "GIPR-specific drug-driven GDF15 induction" reading is a hypothesis generated by this dataset and a well-motivated target for serum GDF15 measurement, not a finding established by it. The large baseline elevation, by contrast, is solid.

    Calcium cycling

    The two calcium-handling genes of mechanistic interest do not point the same way. Atp2a2 (SERCA2) is modestly higher in GIPR-Adip fat (log2FC 0.30, padj 0.0098), consistent in direction with a calcium-cycling contribution. Ryr2, however, is significantly lower in GIPR-Adip fat (log2FC -1.77, padj 1.3e-4) — the opposite of the direction a beige-fat calcium-cycling model would predict. We are flagging this as an open, unresolved discrepancy rather than fitting it into a story.

    External concordance

    Against the published baseline GIPR-Adip RNA-seq (PMID 39642881), 13,910 genes are shared between the two result tables. Across all shared genes the effect sizes correlate at Pearson r = 0.54 (Spearman rho = 0.56). Among the 3,268 genes called significant in both datasets, direction agrees 92.6% of the time and effect sizes correlate at r = 0.81, and 73% of the genes significant in the published dataset are also significant here. The baseline genotype signature therefore reproduces well where both datasets have power, with the weaker all-gene correlation reflecting the many low-signal genes where neither study is confident.

    Energy metabolism block (glycolysis / PDH / TCA)

    46 genes spanning glucose uptake, glycolysis, the PDH complex, and the TCA cycle were tested across all four primary contrasts. GIPR-Adip fat runs low on PDH and TCA genes at baseline, and rosiglitazone raises this block mainly in WT fat.

    Caveats — read before citing a single gene

    What this analysis establishes, and what it does not, stated as plainly as we can.

    Firmly supported: adipocyte GIPR overexpression on doxycycline high-fat diet produces a very large, highly reproducible remodelling of the subcutaneous white-fat transcriptome, it reproduces the published baseline GIPR-Adip signature where both datasets have power, and the transgene is robustly induced in all six GIPR-Adip pools.

    A real but secondary layer: the genotype x rosiglitazone interaction is statistically present, but it is an order of magnitude smaller than the baseline genotype effect, and the priority C4 contrast is mostly the baseline genotype difference carried through treatment rather than a drug-created divergence. Any mechanistic claim about rosiglitazone acting through a GIPR-specific pathway rests on the 861-gene interaction set and the 53-gene Tier A short list, not on the ten-thousand-gene contrasts. Genotype separation in a figure is not by itself proof of a drug-specific mechanism.

    Not established — GDF15 causality or genotype-specificity of its drug response. The baseline elevation of Gdf15 in GIPR-Adip fat is unambiguous. The appealing further step — that rosiglitazone specifically amplifies GDF15 in GIPR-Adip fat — is not supported by the formal interaction test, which is not significant. Nothing here speaks to whether adipose Gdf15 transcript translates into circulating GDF15 protein, or whether that would be sufficient to suppress appetite. This is a hypothesis for serum follow-up.

    Not resolved — the calcium-cycling direction. Atp2a2 is modestly higher in GIPR-Adip fat while Ryr2 is significantly lower, which is the opposite of what the proposed beige-fat calcium-cycling route would predict. We have not reconciled these two observations, and we do not recommend building a thermogenic calcium-cycling narrative on them without orthogonal measurement.

    Design-level limits that apply throughout: the unit of replication is the pool, not the mouse, with three pools per group and unequal numbers of mice contributing per pool, so within-group biological variability is partly averaged away and power for the smaller treatment and interaction effects is limited. Pools also differ in skeletal-muscle content, an expected consequence of subcutaneous dissection; the muscle-covariate and composition-adjusted sensitivity models were run precisely to identify which results that affects, and the Tier A list is restricted to genes that survived them. Finally, cell-composition estimates are reference-based and descriptive — GIPR-Adip fat differs in adipocyte, myeloid and stromal signal, so some bulk differences reflect a different tissue composition rather than a different programme within the same cell type. All reported counts come from the primary DESeq2 ~0 + group model; the sensitivity models are reported as checks and give somewhat different totals by design.
